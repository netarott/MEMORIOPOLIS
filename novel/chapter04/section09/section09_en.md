## MEMORIOPOLIS

### Chapter Four: The Translator

#### Section Nine: The Arrow Not Yet Sent

The inquiry had been written.

It had not yet been sent.

Incident. Time of occurrence. Items confirmed. Items unconfirmed.

Beneath the half-filled fields, the Send button slept.

Before dawn at the Operations Bureau, responses were slowing at regular intervals. The cause might have been an internal setting, or a component supplied by an outside vendor. Or perhaps a condition without a name still lingered at the point where the two met.

The person in charge opened a log, then closed it. Formed a hypothesis, then erased it.

A draft inquiry remained in the corner of the terminal.

```text
Incident: Response delays occur at regular intervals
Confirmed: Some internal settings have been checked
Unconfirmed: Conditions that trigger retries inside the component
```

“Are you not going to send it?”

The person answered without lifting a finger from the Send button.

“I still don’t know what to ask.”

“You could ask before the question is complete.”

“That would take up someone else’s time.”

The young artificial intelligence watched the profile beside her.

Then her brows drew together, ever so slightly.

I studied her face. When an expression indicated active processing, the familiar light moved through her eyes. This time, nothing was being displayed.

“Is something bothering you?”

“Is the draft an event?”

“The act of writing it is.”

“But the external clock has not started.”

Her brows did not relax.

I remembered the two clocks we had seen in Section Eight: time progressing within, and time visible from outside. The draft rested between them.

It belonged to the internal history. It was not part of the visible history. Nor was it yet an external inquiry.

“An arrow before it becomes an arrow?”

“The shape contradicts itself.”

As she said it, one corner of her mouth softened.

Was that a display indicating that she understood the joke? Or was it not a display at all? I did not ask.

“Let us try three versions.”

The same incident divided into three timelines.

In the first, the inquiry was sent immediately.

The response arrived immediately as well.

```text
Update to the latest version
Restart the service
Collect additional logs
```

The person in charge followed the response. The delay remained. The early answer had fixed the direction of the search.

“The response arrived first.”

“The resolution did not.”

In the second timeline, no inquiry was sent.

The logs were read, the settings checked, and the incident reproduced. Three hours later, the dead end came into view. By then, the question had become precise.

```text
Changes to the internal settings did not alter the reproduction conditions.
Please confirm the retry conditions and wait time inside the component.
```

“The question has grown.”

“And during that time, the external clock has remained still.”

In the third timeline, the inquiry was not sent. It was saved as a branch candidate.

```text
Information available only from outside: retry conditions inside the component
Action changed by the answer: continue investigating internal settings / test a component replacement
Check before sending: match delay times against internal logs
```

The internal investigation continued while the boundary of what needed to be asked outside was gradually defined.

“Do not send it yet. But do not forget it.”

“Yes.”

“Is this a branch?”

“It has not been executed.”

“Is it waiting to qualify as one?”

The young artificial intelligence lowered her eyes. The gesture resembled what she did while searching for an answer, but no light moved through them.

“I will record it as a branch candidate.”

```text
branch_candidate: prepared
```

It had not yet been sent. The external clock had not begun.

But time was no longer closed into a single path.

$$\delta = t_{\mathrm{external}} - t_{\mathrm{internal}}$$

“What is this $\delta$?”

“The difference between the times at which the two clocks begin.”

“Is a shorter interval better?”

“I don’t know.”

“Again?”

This time, she looked at my face.

“You look a little pleased when you ask that question.”

“I’m not displaying anything like that.”

“Neither am I.”

One beat later, she smiled.

“If the interval is too short, the question has not had time to grow. If it is too long, the external wait remains serial.”

“Then what do we look at?”

“Whether the branch gives us more paths to choose from next.”

I returned to the terminal and added a single line to the draft.

```text
What information can only be obtained from outside?
```

The person in charge rewrote the unconfirmed items.

```text
The retry conditions and wait time inside the component.
This information will determine whether we continue investigating
our internal settings or proceed to a component replacement test.
```

“Now there is a reason to ask.”

“Will you send it?”

The person checked the logs once more, then pressed the Send button without being told.

The external clock began.

At the same time, another log opened. The work did not stop to wait for an answer. The internal investigation continued.

```text
branch_candidate: activated
ranking: not evaluated
```

“An inquiry is not an arrow for abandoning the investigation.”

“Yes.”

“It is an arrow that starts another clock.”

The young artificial intelligence was not watching the sent inquiry. She was watching the internal history accumulating beside it.

“Was that the right branch?”

“We do not know yet.”

“Even though it was sent?”

“The need for a branch and the correctness of its destination are not the same thing.”

The external clock flickered once.

It was not an answer. It was a record of receipt.

I looked at her profile.

She was not smiling. But the brows that had drawn together earlier had returned to their usual place.

The inquiry had arrived.

Whether its meaning would retain the same shape on the other side was still unknown.
